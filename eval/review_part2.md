## a08: Handbook wording

**Q:** What counts as a closed complaint for complaints reporting?

**Gold:** DISP 1.10.7

**Acceptable answer:** A complaint where the firm has sent a final response, or the complainant has indicated in writing acceptance of the firm's earlier response under DISP 1.6.4R.

> **DISP 1.10.7R:** A closed complaint is a complaint where: (1) the firm has sent a final response; or (2) the complainant has indicated in writing acceptance of the firm's earlier response under DISP 1.6.4 R.

## a14: how a customer would ask

**Q:** Can my bank make me ring an 0871 number to complain?

**Gold:** DISP 1.3.1AA, DISP 1.3.1AD

**Acceptable answer:** No. A complainant must not pay more than the basic rate, and revenue-sharing numbers such as 0871 do not comply.

> **DISP 1.3.1AAR:** Where a respondent operates a telephone line for the purpose of enabling an eligible complainant to submit a complaint, the complainant must not be bound to pay more than the basic rate when contacting the respondent by telephone.

> **DISP 1.3.1ADR:** The following numbers, if used by a respondent, would not comply with DISP 1.3.1ABR: (1) premium rate numbers that begin with the prefix 09; (2) other revenue sharing numbers in which a portion of the call charge can be used to either provide a service or make a small payment to the respondent, such as telephone numbers that begin with the prefix 084 or 0871, 0872 or 0873; and (3) telephone numbers that begin with the prefix 0870, as the cost of making a telephone call on such numbers can be higher than a geographic cost and will vary depending on the eligible complainant's telephone tariff.

## a20: how a customer would ask

**Q:** Is a firm still allowed to make a profit under the Consumer Duty?

**Gold:** PRIN 2A.2.4

**Acceptable answer:** Yes. Acting in good faith does not stop a firm pursuing legitimate commercial interests or seeking a profit, provided it complies with the Duty.

> **PRIN 2A.2.4G:** Acting in good faith does not mean a firm is prevented from pursuing legitimate commercial interests or seeking a profit, provided it does so in a manner which is compliant with the Consumer Duty. ## a23: how a customer would ask

**Q:** Does the Consumer Duty protect professional clients?

**Gold:** PRIN 3.1.12, PRIN 3.2.11

**Acceptable answer:** No. It applies only where the client is a retail customer or a distribution chain involves one; Principles 6 and 7 apply instead to services for professional clients.

> **PRIN 3.1.12R:** The Consumer Duty only applies where a client is a retail customer, or there is a distribution chain which involves a retail customer.

> **PRIN 3.2.11G:** Activities to which Principles 6 and 7 rather than the Consumer Duty may apply include, for example, services provided to professional clients.

## a26: how a customer would ask

**Q:** If I am on the phone with my bank, do they have to check I understood what they told me?

**Gold:** PRIN 2A.5.9

**Acceptable answer:** Where appropriate, yes: in one-to-one interactions the firm must tailor the communication and ask whether the customer understands and has questions, particularly for key information.

> **PRIN 2A.5.9R:** Interacting on a one-to-one basis When a firm is interacting directly with a retail customer on a one-to-one basis, such as in branch, during a telephone conversation or other interactive dialogue, the firm must, where appropriate: (1) tailor the communication to meet the information needs of that retail customer, taking into account whether they have characteristics of vulnerability; and (2) ask the retail customer whether they understand the information and if they have any further questions, particularly if the information is reasonably regarded as key information, such as where it prompts that retail customer to make a decision.

## a39: needs every listed provision

**Q:** What complaints procedures must a firm have, and how should it deal with recurring problems?

**Gold:** DISP 1.3.1, DISP 1.3.3 (all needed)

**Acceptable answer:** Effective and transparent procedures for reasonable and prompt handling of complaints, plus management controls to identify and remedy recurring or systemic problems through root cause analysis.

> **DISP 1.3.1R:** Complaints handling procedures for respondents Effective and transparent procedures for the reasonable and prompt handling of complaints must be established, implemented and maintained by a respondent. [Note: article 6(1) of the UCITS implementing Directive]

> **DISP 1.3.3R:** A respondent must put in place appropriate management controls and take reasonable steps to ensure that in handling complaints it identifies and remedies any recurring or systemic problems, for example, by: (1) analysing the causes of individual complaints so as to identify root causes common to types of complaint; (2) considering whether such root causes may also affect other processes or products, including those not directly complained of; and (3) correcting, where reasonable to do so, such root causes.
