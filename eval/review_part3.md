## a06: Handbook wording

**Q:** How does the price and value outcome define fair value?

**Gold:** PRIN 2A.4.1

**Acceptable answer:** Value is the relationship between the amount a retail customer pays and the benefits they can reasonably expect; a product provides fair value where the amount paid is reasonable relative to its benefits.

> **PRIN 2A.4.1R:** What is value? For the purposes of this outcome: (1) value is the relationship between the amount paid by a retail customer for the product and the benefits they can reasonably expect to get from the product; and (2) a product provides fair value where the amount paid for the product is reasonable relative to the benefits of the product.

## a09: Handbook wording

**Q:** Does a contravention of the rules in PRIN give rise to a right of action by a private person under section 138D of the Act?

**Gold:** PRIN 3.4.4

**Acceptable answer:** No. Each PRIN rule is specified under section 138D(3) as giving rise to no such right of action.

> **PRIN 3.4.4R:** Actions for damages A contravention of the rules in PRIN does not give rise to a right of action by a private person under section 138D of the Act (and each of those rules is specified under section 138D(3) of the Act as a provision giving rise to no such right of action).

## a12: Handbook wording

**Q:** For how long must a firm retain the record of each complaint it receives?

**Gold:** DISP 1.9.1

**Acceptable answer:** Three years from receipt for most complaints; at least five years where the complaint relates to collective portfolio management services for a UCITS scheme.

> **DISP 1.9.1R:** A firm, a payment service provider or an e-money issuer, must keep a record of each complaint received and the measures taken for its resolution, and retain that record for: (1) at least five years where the complaint relates to collective portfolio management services for a UCITS scheme or a scheme which, immediately before IP completion day, was an EEA UCITS scheme; and (2) three years for all other complaints; from the date the complaint was received.

## a15: how a customer would ask

**Q:** My complaint is really about another company's mistake. Can the firm pass it on to them?

**Gold:** DISP 1.7.1

**Acceptable answer:** Yes, if it has reasonable grounds to think the other firm is solely or jointly responsible. It must forward it promptly in writing, tell the complainant promptly in a final response why and give the other firm's contact details, and deal with any part it is jointly responsible for.

> **DISP 1.7.1R:** Forwarding a complaint A respondent that has reasonable grounds to be satisfied that another respondent may be solely or jointly responsible for the matter alleged in a complaint may forward the complaint, or the relevant part of it, in writing to that other respondent, provided it: (1) does so promptly; (2) informs the complainant promptly in a final response of why the complaint has been forwarded by it to the other respondent, and of the other respondent's contact details; and (3) where jointly responsible for the fault alleged in the complaint, it complies with its own obligations under this chapter in respect of that part of the complaint it has not forwarded.

## a27: how a customer would ask

**Q:** If an investment firm sorts out a MiFID complaint within three days, does it still have to follow the full time limit rules?

**Gold:** DISP 1.1A.23

**Acceptable answer:** No. If resolved by close of business on the third business day it may comply with DISP 1.1A.24R to 1.1A.27G instead of 1.1A.28R to 1.1A.34G.

> **DISP 1.1A.23R:** Complaints resolved by close of the third business day If a MiFID investment firm resolves a MiFID complaint by close of business on the third business day following the dayon which it is received, it may choose to comply with DISP 1.1A.24R to DISP 1.1A.27G rather than with DISP 1.1A.28R to DISP 1.1A.34G.

## a31: needs every listed provision

**Q:** What periods do a firm's complaints reports cover, and when are they due?

**Gold:** DISP 1.10.4, DISP 1.10.5 (all needed)

**Acceptable answer:** The six months immediately after and the six months immediately before the firm's accounting reference date (unless the annual rule applies); each report is due within 30 business days of the period's end.

> **DISP 1.10.4R:** Unless DISP 1.10.4AR applies, the relevant reporting periods are: (1) the six months immediately following a firm's accounting reference date; and (2) the six months immediately preceding a firm's accounting reference date.

> **DISP 1.10.5R:** Reports are to be submitted to the FCA within 30 business days of the end of the relevant reporting periods through, and in the electronic format specified in, the FCA Complaints Reporting System or the appropriate section of the FCA website.

## a34: needs every listed provision

**Q:** What report must go to a firm's governing body under the Consumer Duty, and what must the governing body do with it?

**Gold:** PRIN 2A.8.3, PRIN 2A.8.4 (all needed)

**Acceptable answer:** A report on the results of its outcomes monitoring and any resulting actions. At least annually the governing body must review and approve it, confirm whether the firm complies with the Duty, and assess whether future strategy is consistent with it.

> **PRIN 2A.8.3R:** Governing body report A firm must prepare a report for its governing body setting out the results of its monitoring under PRIN 2A.9 and any actions required as a result of the monitoring.

> **PRIN 2A.8.4R:** At least annually, the governing body of a firm must: (1) review and approve the firm’s report on the outcomes being received by retail customers; (2) confirm whether it is satisfied that the firm is complying with its obligations under the Consumer Duty; and (3) assess whether the firm’s future business strategy is consistent with its obligations under the Consumer Duty.

## a40: needs every listed provision

**Gold:** PRIN 1.1.7, PRIN 3.4.4 (all needed)

**Acceptable answer:** The firm is liable to disciplinary sanctions, with the onus on the FCA to show fault; but a breach of PRIN gives no private right of action under section 138D.

Under each of the Principles the onus will be on the FCA to show that a firm or other person has been at fault in some way. What constitutes "fault" varies between different Principles.
